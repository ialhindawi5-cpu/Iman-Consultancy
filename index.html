The problem

Most projects don't fail on skill. They fail on follow-up.

The work is understood, the team is capable — and yet the deadline moves. Usually because nobody owns the space between the tasks: the handovers, the approvals sitting in someone's inbox, the risk everyone saw coming but no one logged.

That space is what I own. I put a plan behind the intent, a cadence behind the plan, and a single source of truth behind the status — so the project stops depending on who remembered what.

Approach

How an engagement runs

Same four beats every time, whether it's a six-week rollout or a year-long programme.

  1. 01

    Discover

    We map the goal, the constraints, every stakeholder who can say no, and what "done" actually means. You get a written scope before anyone commits.

  2. 02

    Plan

    A work breakdown with an owner on every task, a timeline built from real capacity rather than optimism, and a risk log with mitigations already assigned.

  3. 03

    Coordinate

    I run the cadence — standups, follow-ups, vendor chasing, approvals unblocked. Nothing sits waiting on someone remembering to send it.

  4. 04

    Report

    One dashboard and one weekly note: progress against plan, spend, live risks, and the decisions that need you this week. No surprises at the deadline.

Questions

Before you get in touch

Do you work remotely?

Yes — most engagements are fully remote, with working hours overlapping your team. I'm based in Beirut and can travel for kickoffs or key milestones where it matters.

What size of project do you take on?

Anything from a single six-week rollout to a portfolio of parallel workstreams. If a project is too small to need me, I'll tell you that in the first call.

Can you work inside our existing tools?

Almost always. I work in Jira, MS Project, Zoho, Asana, Odoo, Trello and plain spreadsheets. If your process is working, I fit into it rather than replacing it.

How does an engagement start?

A 30-minute call to understand the project and the constraints, then a short written proposal: scope, cadence, deliverables and cost. No obligation on either side until that's agreed.
